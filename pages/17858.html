Caption

GitHub is experimenting with a different way to build coding agents. Instead of forcing every task through the same model, HydraFusion decides how a task should be solved and can dynamically use one model, escalate to another, or bring in a second model to critique the first. It has three main workflows: **SINGLE** One model handles the task. **CASCADE** A cheaper model tries first, then a stronger model takes over if the result doesn't meet the quality bar. **CRITIQUE** One model writes the solution, another reviews it, and the first model revises. And the headline result is interesting: 67% lower estimated workflow cost + 4.9 points higher verified quality than Claude Opus 5 on TerminalBench 2.1, according to GitHub's reported offline evaluation. But there's an important catch: HydraFusion didn't beat Opus 5 on quality across every benchmark. On DeepSWE it was 1.5 points lower while costing 36% less, and on CheckpointBench it was 0.1 points lower while costing 65% less. So the bigger idea isn't simply “multiple models = better.” It's: the AI model may no longer be the whole product.
