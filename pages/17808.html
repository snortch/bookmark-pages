Slash goal is fundamentally a looping mechanism that causes your agent to continually loop on the same prompt until it achieves a certain outcome. If you're still just doing slash goal and putting your prompt after it, you are missing so much power from this feature. By just raw prompting your slash goal command, you're allowing the agent to decide the exact steps it takes in those loops within slash goal. What you want to do is take a task management system, whatever it might be, whether it's linear or notion for you, or literally just markdown files, one for each task, and then in your slash goal command, actually define the procedure that the agent will take to iterate through those tasks over a really long period of time. Now you're actually controlling the procedure of the decisions that the agent makes during its slash goal loops. So it will get much more granular, follow your instructions much more exactly, and you can actually control how long that slash goal loop goes for by how many tasks you include in that goal. Since the slash goal feature allows you to know that your agent won't stop working until it achieves that goal for you, why not specify as granularly as possible the exact procedure and exactly how many steps it must take to achieve that goal so you can force it to work for days on end.
